# Teacher-query playbook

Use the smallest retrieval pattern that answers the teacher's actual question. Each curriculum-specific answer must include archive citations and verified, shareable public source links as described in `citation-guidance.md`.

## Exact resource or lesson lookup

Browse for canonical grade and unit metadata, then search with the exact lesson and artifact kind. Return the strongest match first with title, grade, unit, lesson, artifact type, archive citation, and public source page. Explain close matches rather than silently choosing one.

## Recalled activity navigation

Search the teacher's remembered concrete details: phenomenon, object, quoted phrase, data set, student action, or discussion move. Compare a few results and read enough surrounding context to identify the lesson and where the activity falls in the sequence. State confidence and the clues used.

## Materials and lab preparation

Prefer teacher editions, material lists, and preparation notes. Separate consumables, reusable equipment, advance preparation, safety or cleanup, and quantities only when the source supports them. Flag missing quantities or local substitutions instead of inventing them.

## Unit or lesson summaries

Inspect both the target material and adjacent sequence context. Summarize the phenomenon or question, what students do, evidence or ideas developed, and what the work prepares students to do next. Keep source facts distinct from synthesis.

## DQB and discussion preparation

Find the relevant teacher guidance, prompts, anticipated student ideas, and purpose in the lesson sequence. Provide a brief facilitation preparation view: purpose, opening move, ideas to notice, possible follow-ups, and intended landing point. Do not script over student sensemaking or present anticipated responses as required answers.

## Differentiation, substitute, and first-year support

Retrieve the original task and teacher guidance first. Offer bounded supports such as vocabulary preview, chunking, representation choices, participation structures, timing landmarks, and teacher look-fors. Preserve the phenomenon, investigation, evidence-building, and discussion sequence. Label any support not stated in the curriculum as an adaptation.

For substitute support, distinguish what must remain faithful from what can be simplified operationally. For a first-year teacher, explain hidden dependencies and likely preparation points without replacing professional judgment.

## Terminology

Find the term in its lesson context. Explain its curriculum-specific meaning, when students encounter it, and how it supports the current sensemaking. Do not replace the curriculum's contextual use with a generic dictionary definition.

## Assessment and rubric guidance

Prefer the assessment, rubric, teacher edition, and answer key appropriate to the educator's request. Explain the assessed idea or practice, evidence to look for, and relevant rubric distinctions. Do not expose secure answers to students or imply that one excerpt defines the full scoring policy.

## Faithful student-instruction rewrites

Before rewriting, retrieve the original student directions and any necessary teacher context. Preserve:

- the scientific meaning and phenomenon;
- required evidence, data, materials, and constraints;
- task order and dependencies;
- student choice, collaboration, or discussion expectations; and
- the requested student product or response.

Change only the requested feature, such as reading level, length, formatting, language, or accessibility. Do not add an answer, remove productive uncertainty, or turn sensemaking into recall. Present the rewrite with a short fidelity note listing any intentional changes. Cite the original directions and include their verified public source page.
